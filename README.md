## The joke

> On Monday, even my shadow clocks in late.

41 characters, one line, no trailing newline.

## Why it should land

The bounty asks for one joke about Monday mornings, and the judge
weighs a real laugh first. This line starts from the most familiar
Monday feeling there is — dragging yourself to work late — and then
hands the blame to the one thing that can never be late, because it
is physically attached to you. A shadow has no alarm to snooze, no
traffic to blame, no bed to stay in; it arrives exactly when you
do. Making it clock in late anyway is absurd, and that absurdity
is the punchline: even the part of you that cannot escape Monday
is trying to.

There is no coffee, no alarm clock, no groan about hating Mondays
and no borrowed cartoon cat. The image is new — a shadow punching
a time card — and it is short enough to read in one breath: nine
words, and every one of them is doing work. At 41 characters it
is also on the short side, and short text wins an exact tie.
